# Kavach — pitch card

Read this on your phone at the venue. Numbers first, adjectives never.

---

## The 20-second opener

> "A family at a discharge counter gets a bill with forty line items and twenty minutes to decide if it's right. Nobody in that room can tell which rupees are lawful. The TPA has already adjudicated. The paper says 'non-payable' and everyone assumes it is.
>
> Kavach tells you which rupees are *not*. It re-does the claim arithmetic against IRDAI's own rules, and hands you a letter that quotes the clause back."

---

## The one thing that makes this real

Room-rent proportionate deduction exists because a deluxe room costs more than a twin-sharing room. That logic applies to **room, surgeon, and OT charges** — things whose price scales with the room.

It does **not** apply to a strip of antibiotics. Pharmacy, implants and diagnostics cost the hospital exactly the same in any room class. IRDAI's proportionate-deduction norms say this in terms: those three heads are barred from the "associated medical expenses" base.

TPAs apply the ratio anyway. That's a computable, citable, recoverable amount — on every bill.

**Say this:** *"The ratio is lawful on three lines. It was applied to five."*

## Q&A — the four that will come

**"Why would a TPA care about your letter?"**
They don't care about the letter. They care about the 14-day clock and the Ombudsman. Reg 17 of the PPI Regulations 2017 requires closure in 14 days; silence is itself a breach. The letter is a paper trail that makes escalation cheap.

**"Isn't this legal advice?"**
No. It's a factual objection that points at a document. It names a rupee, a line, and a clause. It doesn't tell anyone what to do medically or legally, and the app says so on screen.

**"Didn't IRDAI repeal the proportionate-deduction norms in 2024?"**
Yes — Annexure-6, entry 19 of the 29 May 2024 Master Circular. That's why the argument rests on three independent legs: the clause reproduced in the policy wording (which is the contract), the 2019 itemisation lists (still operative), and *New India Assurance v. A.K. Kariappa* (NCDRC R.P. 766/2011), where the Commission called flat-ratio deduction "faulty". No single repeal collapses it.

**"Medi Assist already has this data — why you?"**
They have the data and a conflict of interest: they're paid by the insurer to adjudicate. Kavach sits on the patient's side of the table. Same arithmetic, opposite incentive.

## Landscape — what already exists

A judge who has done homework will know some of this. Saying it first is worth more than pretending otherwise.

| Product | What it does | What it doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| **BillOkay** (India) | Photo → audit against CGHS / NPPA / PMJAY rate cards → dispute letter, on WhatsApp. Free at launch. | Audits the *hospital's prices* against government rate cards. Never reads your policy or a TPA deduction sheet. |
| **Drapto / ClaimAuditLab** (India) | Publish the proportionate-deduction arithmetic head-by-head, citing 151/06/2020. Free. | Generic calculators on self-entered data. No OCR, no policy ingestion, no letter, no consumer distribution. |
| **Insurance Samadhan** (India) | Human-run claim grievance recovery, success fee. | After the fact, human-heavy, no line-item arithmetic. |
| **ClaimBuddy** (India) | Humans stationed at hospital insurance desks. | Service, not software. No clause-level audit. |
| **Medi Assist / Paramount / Vidal** | TPA-side adjudication at scale — 8.9m+ claims, ₹21,108 cr premium under management (FY25). | They *are* the deduction. No incentive to audit it. |
| **Goodbill / Resolve** (US) | Patient-side bill negotiation, 20–25% of savings. | Chargemaster and upcoding disputes, not insurance-contract disputes. Economics don't transfer to a ₹31,000 average Indian claim. |
| **Claimable / Fight Health Insurance** (US) | AI-drafted denial appeals, $39.95 flat / free. | Denial-and-appeal shaped: argues medical necessity, never audits line arithmetic. |

**The gap, stated narrowly:** every consumer tool audits the **bill** against a rate card. None audits the **adjudication** against the policy. Those are different documents, different counterparties, and different remedies — "your hospital is expensive" versus "your insurer's arithmetic departs from the clause it filed".

**Do not say "nobody has built this."** Say: *"BillOkay tells you the hospital overcharged. We tell you the insurer's deduction has no clause. Different defendant."*

## What NOT to claim

Protect yourself. These are the sentences that get you caught.

| Don't say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "This is illegal" | "This has no clause behind it" / "not payable under clause X" |
| "Certified dossier" | "Clause-linked dossier" — nothing certifies a ₹49 PDF |
| "IRDAI mandates this today" | "The policy wording carries this standard" |
| "These are real patient cases" | "Illustrative composites — the arithmetic is exact" |
| "We'll get your money back" | "We give you the document that starts the clock" |
| "AI reads your bill perfectly" | "AI reads it, you correct it, the arithmetic is checked" |
| "Nobody has built this" | "BillOkay audits the bill; we audit the adjudication" |

## The honest limitation, owned out loud

> "The three cases are composites. The camera path is real but OCR will sometimes misread a figure, which is why every line is editable and there's an arithmetic-mismatch detector. The rules are encoded from primary sources — and where a circular has been repealed, the citation says so."

Saying this before a judge finds it is worth more than any feature.
